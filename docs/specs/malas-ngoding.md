# Malas Ngoding: 3D energy digital twin of Kalimantan Timur

Design spec, 20 Oct 2026. Team and app name: **Malas Ngoding**.

## Why

Entry for the INSEVENT 2026 Fullstack Web Development Competition (Sistem Informasi, Universitas Mulawarman), subtheme **SDG 7, Integrated Clean Energy Ecosystem**. Submission deadline: **25 Oct 2026, 23.59 WITA**.

Most of Kaltim's electricity comes from coal. Ordinary people cannot see how much CO2 their buildings cause, or what moving to rooftop solar would change. Malas Ngoding is an interactive 3D map that makes this visible and understandable for anyone, and gives local government data-driven recommendations.

Scoring this design optimises for:
- First round: innovation 30%, UI/UX 25%, technical implementation 25%, documentation and impact 20%.
- Final: **live demo 50%**, technical depth (tech, architecture, security) 30%, presentation 20%.

So: a stunning, smooth, animated live demo comes first. Technical depth (PostGIS, caching, AI tool use) comes second.

## Goals

1. A full-screen 3D Mapbox map of Kalimantan Timur (style Mapbox Standard, terrain on). The 10 kabupaten/kota are extruded by their electricity use or CO2, coloured by intensity, hoverable and clickable.
2. **Click any building** (Mapbox Standard `buildings` featureset, via `map.addInteraction`) to see:
   - estimated yearly and monthly kWh (from footprint area x floors x intensity for the building type),
   - the grid mix behind that electricity (e.g. 70% coal PLTU, 25% gas), not one specific plant, because grid power is pooled,
   - estimated CO2 per year,
   - layman equivalents (car km, trees needed, phone charges, flights).
   Every estimated number is labelled "perkiraan".
3. **Solar simulation** for the selected building: roof area -> kWp, yearly production (NASA POWER irradiance), share of demand covered, battery size for N hours of backup, cost, payback years, CO2 avoided.
4. **Weather-aware solar prediction**: Open-Meteo forecast for the next days, predicted PV output per hour/day, with rain and cloud lowering output. The map shows a live rain effect (`map.setRain`) when it rains at the view centre, and the light preset follows the real time of day in WITA.
5. **Power plants layer**: major Kaltim plants as 3D markers coloured by fuel, sized by MW.
6. **Recommendations for government** per kabupaten/kota (sustainable development and spatial planning), generated by Claude from the region's data and cached in the database, with a rule-based fallback.
7. **AI chat** (Anthropic API, model `claude-haiku-5-5`, no rate limit) that answers questions about the map and **drives the map**: fly to a region, select a building at a place, toggle layers, run the solar simulation, toggle rain. Map actions are tool calls that the client executes.
8. **Animations everywhere** ("kita main di animasi"): intro fly-in from globe to Kaltim, regions rising, smooth camera moves, panel transitions, animated counters and charts, hover glow, slow orbit when idle.

## Non-goals

- No real metered data, IoT or sensors. All per-building numbers are model estimates.
- No user accounts or login.
- No deployment yet (Railway later, after the user reviews). The app must still be deploy-ready (Dockerfile or simple start command).
- No mobile-first layout; desktop and laptop for judges come first, but the layout must not break on a tablet.

## Anti-goals (failure even if it works)

- A demo that stutters, shows blank panels, or throws errors in the live demo.
- Numbers that look invented without a source or method. Every factor must trace to `docs/research/*.md` or be marked as an assumption.
- AI chat that hallucinates numbers: the chat must get numbers from tools that query our data.
- Any Claude/AI attribution in commits or docs.

## Constraints

- Runtime: **Bun** everywhere (server, bundler, tests, scripts). Frontend **React** + TypeScript.
- Database: **Postgres + PostGIS** in Docker for local dev, on host port **5433** (port 5432 already runs another project's container; never touch it). Access via `Bun.sql`.
- Secrets in `.env` (`MAPBOX_TOKEN`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `DATABASE_URL`). Never commit `.env`; `.env.example` documents keys. The Mapbox public token is served to the client through `/api/config`; the Anthropic key never leaves the server.
- API routes under `/api/...` without version prefix.
- UI language: **Bahasa Indonesia**. Code, identifiers and comments in English.
- Git: branch `main` only, no PRs. Commit format `type(scope): desc` (conventional commits, lower case, e.g. `feat(map): extrude regions by co2`). No attribution trailers.
- External APIs (NASA POWER, Open-Meteo) are cached in the database so the live demo works on slow wifi; seed data ships with the repo so the app runs offline except for map tiles and Claude.

## Data and method

Research files in `docs/research/` hold the numbers and sources:
- `grid-and-plants.md`: grid emission factor, generation mix, plant list with coordinates, fuel factors.
- `consumption-by-region.md`: kWh sold, customers, population, electrification per kab/kota.
- `building-intensity.md`: kWh/m2/year per building type, OSM tag mapping, floor height, layman equivalents.
- `solar-weather-boundaries.md`: NASA POWER and Open-Meteo endpoints, PV and battery formulas, costs and tariff, ADM2 boundaries source.

Building estimate: `area_m2 = footprint area (from feature geometry, computed server-side with PostGIS or turf)`, `floors`, `kWh/yr = area_m2 x floors x intensity[type]`.

Fact check done 20 Oct (Mapbox Tilequery, `mapbox.mapbox-streets-v8`): Samarinda, Balikpapan, Bontang and Tenggarong all have dense building footprints, but every footprint has `type: "building"` and the default `height: 3`. So neither type nor height is useful from the building itself. Therefore:
- **Type** is inferred from the nearest POI (`poi_label` layer, `class`/`type`, e.g. `education/School`, `store_like/Mall`, `lodging/Hotel`, `general/Government`, `religion/Place Of Worship`) within the footprint or about 25 m of it, looked up server-side via the Tilequery API and cached. Fallback: footprint under ~250 m2 = rumah tinggal, otherwise ruko/komersial.
- **Floors**: if height is the 3 m default, use a typical floor count per type (e.g. house 1, ruko 2-3, school 2, office 4, hotel 6, hospital 4, mall 3); if a real height exists, `round(height / floorHeight)`.
- The user can override type and floors in the panel and the numbers recompute.

CO2: `kWh x grid emission factor`. Breakdown by source: `kWh x mix share x fuel factor`, normalised to the grid factor.

## Architecture (builder may refine)

- `src/server/`: `Bun.serve` with `routes` for `/api/*` and HTML import for the SPA. Modules: `db`, `regions`, `plants`, `buildings` (estimate + cache), `solar` (simulate), `weather` (forecast + cache), `recommendations`, `chat` (Anthropic streaming with tools, SSE to client).
- `src/web/`: React app. Map (Mapbox GL JS v3), panels, chat, animations (Motion for React).
- `src/shared/`: pure calculation modules shared by server and tests (`estimate.ts`, `solar.ts`, `equivalents.ts`, `emissions.ts`) and API types.
- `db/`: SQL migrations; `scripts/seed.ts` loads boundaries GeoJSON and research JSON.
- Tables (minimum): `regions` (geom, stats), `power_plants` (point), `grid_mix`, `fuel_factors`, `building_types`, `building_estimates` (cache by feature id), `solar_climatology_cache`, `weather_cache`, `recommendations`, `chat_sessions`, `chat_messages`.
- Tests: `bun test` for all pure calculation modules and API handlers (handlers with a test database or a thin db fake).
- Docs: `README.md` (local install and run, for judges), `docs/technical/` (ERD and flowcharts in Mermaid, architecture, app flow) as the source for the PDF the team submits.

## Left to the builder

Visual style (aim: modern, dark-first, energetic accent colours, readable for laypeople), exact panel layout, chart library, animation details, chat tool schema, file layout inside `src/`, and test structure. Choose sensible defaults and note them in the spec's decision log below.

## Decision log (calls made without the user, to check on review)

- Repo `akhmadqasim/malas-ngoding`, private for now. The guidebook requires a public repo at submission; switch before 25 Oct.
- Campus and team member names are placeholders in docs.
- The grid mix is shown per building instead of a single plant, because grid electricity cannot be traced to one plant.
- Research files are being produced by research agents on 20 Oct. Any value without a solid source goes in as an explicit assumption (in the seed data with `source = 'asumsi'` and shown in an "Metodologi & sumber" panel), never silently.
- **Build order** (everything in Goals is in scope; this is the cut order if time runs out, last cut first): (1) map + regions + intro animation, (2) building click + estimate + equivalents, (3) solar simulation, (4) AI chat with map actions, (5) weather forecast + rain effect, (6) recommendations, (7) power plants layer. Animation polish is part of every step, not a final step.
- Verification: builders are agents. Every step ships with `bun test` coverage for logic and handlers, plus a browser smoke check of the real app (map renders, click works, no console errors) before the step counts as done. A scripted demo path is documented in `docs/technical/demo-script.md`.
- Demo setup is one command sequence in the README: `docker compose up -d`, `bun install`, `bun run setup` (migrate + seed + warm caches), `bun dev`. The final live demo runs on the team laptop; judges in round one will use a deployed URL that the team creates later.
- Chat guardrails: max 8 tool-loop iterations per user message, `max_tokens` around 2048 per response, history kept per anonymous session id (stored in localStorage, persisted in `chat_sessions`/`chat_messages`).
- Region recommendations are generated for all 10 regions during `bun run setup` (when the key is present) and cached; a "Perbarui" button regenerates on demand. Solar climatology for each region centroid is pre-cached during setup too.
- **Visual direction "Atlas malam"** (Task 6 fix round, 21 Oct; replaces "Mahakam malam", which the user judged AI-ish): binding rules in [`docs/research/design/00-direction.md`](../research/design/00-direction.md), drawn from the ten reports in `docs/research/design/`. In short: a printed atlas read at night. Solid panels with hairlines (paper by day, ink at night) (no glass, glow or gradients on chrome), HeroUI's system font stack for all chrome text (SF on macOS, Segoe UI on Windows; user decision in round 5) with tabular figures (names drawn on the map use DIN Pro, the basemap's own face), colour reserved for data (CO2 amber, kWh teal, six-stop single-hue ramps), one luminance accent for state, a quiet italic "perkiraan" after units, and one signature: the legend is a meter whose needle swings to the region under the pointer. Every raw value lives in the `@theme` block of `src/web/styles.css` and `tests/web/tokens.test.ts` enforces it. Rain appears only from zoom 15 (building scale) as thin light streaks; by day they fall through a slate haze drawn in the map. At the province view our own region names sit on the columns in place of the basemap place names. The light follows WITA time; a "Cahaya: Otomatis / Siang / Malam" switch in the legend (and `?cahaya=`) lets a presenter pin it. Standard theme `faded` in every light, with only Standard's day and night light (dawn and dusk use them; their own presets washed the map tan), and a second (day) ramp per metric so the highest value always has the most contrast (every class at least 3:1, measured from the render). Kept from Task 6: the sheet on the left, the right side free, and the intro as the one orchestrated moment; since round 5 the panels follow the ground (paper with ink type by day, ink with paper type at night), the legend and the map controls are two cards at the bottom, and our own map tools sit bottom-right.
- **The idle orbit is a sway** (Task 6 fix round 4, controller ruling): Goal 8's "slow orbit when idle" is a slow pendulum, 12° either side of the bearing the camera rests at, not a full turn. The province stays readable while a presenter talks, and all ten region names hold over the whole arc (smoke-checked at 1280 to 1920 px, CO2 and kWh); the names of the four largest regions never yield.
- Power plants are a symbol layer with canvas marker images and `icon-occlusion-opacity: 1`, not a circle layer: circles are hidden behind the tall region columns. Since round 5 the markers are flat fuel-coloured dots with thin paper and ink edges (the pulse rings read as targets), and plants that would overlap on screen merge into a cluster marker with their count. Region heights use the square root of the metric so Mahakam Ulu stays visible, and they flatten out between zoom 6.6 and 12 so city buildings take over when zoomed in. Two placeholder plant points that fell in the sea (AlamTri, Balikpapan Refinery) were moved onto land; both stay `approx: true`.
- **UI components: HeroUI v3** (Task 6.5, user decision, 21 Oct): the chrome uses HeroUI v3 components (Card, Button, ToggleButtonGroup, Switch, Toolbar, Tooltip, Popover) styled through a token bridge (`src/web/atlas-theme.css`) that maps the design tokens onto HeroUI's CSS variables per `data-theme`. The panels follow the ground: paper with ink type by day and dawn, ink with paper type at dusk and night. Our own map tools replace Mapbox's zoom and compass control; the Mapbox wordmark and attribution stay visible.
- **Province rooftop solar scenario** (22 Oct, after the UI review): a "Skenario PLTS atap" button top right opens a scenario in the left sheet. A slider (0 to 30%) sets the share of each region's electricity that comes from rooftop PV used on site; `src/shared/scenario.ts` computes per region the solar kWh, kWp (NASA POWER sun hours at the region's label point, `GET /api/scenario`), CO2 avoided (the Kepmen PLTS factor 1.13 kg/kWh, never more than the region's CO2), cost (Rp 15 jt per kWp) and solar's new share of the grid. The CO2 columns sink and pale by the share of CO2 left (`setRegionScale`, scaled against the unscaled maximum so the tallest column sinks too); kWh columns stay, since solar used on site is still electricity used. While the Surya tab of a building is open, the selected building turns the solar fuel colour.
- **Digital twin day** (22 Oct, user decision "mode waktu lengkap"): a "Kembar digital 24 jam" button top right opens the province's next 24 hours in the left sheet. `GET /api/twin` reads the Open-Meteo forecast at every region's label point (three requests at a time, cached an hour; a region whose forecast fails borrows the nearest region's, named in the sheet) from the current WITA hour. `src/shared/twin.ts` spreads each region's yearly kWh over a daily load curve (`data/seed/load-profile.json`, `source: asumsi`: no hourly Kaltim data was found; normalised to a mean of 1), adds rooftop solar from the forecast irradiance (none, or the scenario's kWp), and gives grid MW and CO2 per hour. The CO2 columns scale by the hour's CO2 against the region's average hour (`setRegionScale`), the map light follows the hour (`twinTime`), and "Putar 24 jam" plays the day at 700 ms per hour. The forecast is read again every 10 minutes while open.
- **Renewable potential map** (22 Oct, user request "rekomendasi panel surya dimana atau kincir angin dimana"): a "Potensi EBT" button opens it in the left sheet. Research (`docs/research/renewable-potential.md`): BMKG's open API is a 3-day forecast only, so long-term potential uses NASA POWER climatology 2001-2025 (`scripts/build-re-potential.ts` builds `data/seed/re-potential.json`: the 1° solar grid, wind from the nearest MERRA-2 point; migration `002_re_potential.sql`, seeded by `bun run seed`). `db.potentialCells` (PostGIS) turns each point into a cell clipped to Kaltim's land, with the region holding most of it and the nearest plant's distance. `src/shared/potential.ts` gives PLTS MWh per MWp and, for a generic 4.5 MW turbine at 100 m (1/7 shear, Rayleigh speeds, cubic curve, 0.85 losses; `data/seed/re-assumptions.json`), its capacity factor. Region columns hide while the cells show. Verdict shown as found: the sun is even (4.68 to 5.38 kWh/m2/day), the wind is too weak for utility turbines everywhere (best about 4.5 m/s at 100 m, capacity factor about 10%, against about 6 m/s needed). Land use, peat, slope and grid distance are not modelled; the sheet says so.
- **Terrain and "Bangun di sini" in 3D** (22 Oct, user decision: slope analysis, build-here simulation, realistic glTF models). Slope: `scripts/build-terrain.ts` reads the open Terrain Tiles (Mapzen terrarium on AWS Open Data, SRTM over Borneo; not Mapbox tiles, whose terms restrict derived datasets) at zoom 10, Horn slope on the Kaltim land of each potential cell, into `data/seed/re-terrain.json` (migration `003_re_terrain.sql`). Solar cells rank by irradiance times the share of land at or below 5°. Build here: a picked cell gets "Bangun PLTS di sini" (1 to 30 MWp, 1.75 ha per MWp, one 11 kWp table model per 11 kWp in north-facing rows) or "Pasang turbin angin di sini" (1 to 10 of 4.5 MW, five rotor diameters apart, rotors spinning at the site's wind against 11 m/s); `src/shared/buildSite.ts` gives land, MWh, CO2 avoided (1.13 kg/kWh) and cost. The models (`public/models/*.glb`, served by `/models/:name`) are generated by `scripts/build-models.ts`, so their licence is ours. The menu of the three province views stays in view and marks the open one (user request).
- **Farm site check** (22 Oct, user report: the 3D farm landed in a settlement): before a farm is drawn, `GET /api/site` (routes/site.ts) asks Mapbox Tilequery (Streets v8: building, landuse, landuse_overlay, water) whether the radius of the largest farm the slider allows holds buildings, settled land use, water, national parks or wetlands, and walks rings of 1.5 to 25 km (8 directions) to the nearest clear spot inside Kaltim. The sheet says how far the farm was moved, or that nothing near is clear. Turbines stand in a compact block (rows of four) so one radius covers them, with a 500 m setback. Mapbox Streets can be incomplete in the interior; official forest and peat maps are still not used.
- **Guided story** (22 Oct, user request: the app needs a flow, seen from residents and from government, as a transition toward SDG 7). "Alur cerita", first in the top-right menu, opens a choice of two tracks (`src/web/story/steps.ts`): *Untuk masyarakat* (listrik kita, gedungmu, panel surya di atap, cuaca, kalau semua ikut, langkahmu) and *Untuk pemerintah* (peta emisi, akses listrik in Kutai Barat, target PLTS atap, operasi 24 jam, lokasi PLTS, kebijakan for Samarinda). Every step carries its phase of the energy transition (Kondisi sekarang, Memahami, Merencanakan transisi, Bertindak), shown as a four-part track, and the SDG targets it serves (7.1, 7.2, 7.3, 7.a, 7.b, 11.3, 12.8, 13.2), and sets the map itself. The story card takes the dock's place at the bottom; the camera and the building panel clear it. Esc, the close button or "Selesai" leave it with the map as the last step left it.
- **Accounts, replacing the "no login" non-goal** (22 Oct, user decision). Residents (warga) sign up, mark their home on the map ("Ini rumah saya"), may enter their PLN kWh or bill (it replaces the estimate) and see the rooftop solar that suits it; the chat knows their home (`get_my_home`). Government (pemda) accounts are made by an admin; they get a dashboard of registered homes per region and the homes of owners who agreed (`consent_map`) as anonymous points, never names or emails. Demo buttons sign in as seeded demo accounts (`DEMO_LOGIN`, 13 demo homes labelled "data demo"). Security: argon2id (`Bun.password`), a sha256-hashed session token in an HttpOnly SameSite=Lax cookie, same-origin checks on writes, rate limits on sign-in and search, role checks on the server, and the costly "Perbarui" recommendations only for pemda. The guided story starts on the account's own track.
- **Landing page and address search** (22 Oct, user: the UI is hard for laypeople, and how does a resident find their house?). Before the intro flight, a landing page over the turning globe asks a plain question and offers: search an address or place, "Pakai lokasi saya" (browser geolocation, nothing stored until saved), "Lihat energi Kaltim" and "Saya dari pemerintah". `GET /api/geocode` merges Mapbox Search Box (housing estates, campuses, shops) and Geocoding v6 (kelurahan, cities) inside Kaltim, in Indonesian, never stored (Mapbox terms); Mapbox has few house-level addresses in Kaltim. A picked place flies to building zoom and opens the building there, or asks the resident to click their roof when the point is on a street. `?langsung` or a second visit in the same tab skips the landing.
- **Demo homes on real footprints** (22 Oct, user request: the government dashboard should look like a real rollout). `scripts/build-demo-homes.ts` downloads building footprints from OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API (ODbL 1.0, attribution "© OpenStreetMap contributors"; not Mapbox data, whose terms forbid storing it), one request per city in a box of about 4 km, caches them in git-ignored `data/cache/osm/`, keeps closed `building=house|residential|detached|semidetached_house|terrace|yes` ways of 40 to 250 m2, and picks 300 homes with a fixed-seed sample spread over a grid: Samarinda 110, Balikpapan 80, Bontang 40, Tenggarong 40, Penajam 30, into `data/seed/demo-homes.json`. The footprints are real; type, floors, monthly kWh (about 55% have one), interest in rooftop PV (about 35%) and consent to the map (about 85%) are synthetic (`asumsi`). `bun run seed` makes one demo resident per home ("Warga Demo N", `demoN@warga.local`, `demo = true`, one shared unusable password hash), removes demo residents no longer in the file, and skips homes that fall outside the Kaltim polygons (4 coastal ones). Every one is labelled "data demo" and counted apart from real registrations; the app must show the ODbL attribution wherever these homes are drawn.
